4.0 EPA Cutoff

When Is Radon Action Required?

The EPA federal action threshold is 4.0 pCi/L. However, public health agencies recommend considering mitigation between 2.0 and 3.9 pCi/L, as there is no safe lower threshold.

Radon Basics

Where does radon gas originate in the ground?

Core Answer: Radon (Rn-222) is an inert, radioactive noble gas created by the natural radioactive breakdown of uranium and radium naturally present in rock and soil across North America.

Because radon is chemically unreactive and has no scent or color, it migrates easily through subterranean rock fissures, soil pores, and gravel beds, entering foundations through microscopic shrinkage cracks and expansion seams via building stack effect suction.
Health Risk

Why is radon hazardous if you cannot smell or feel it?

Core Answer: Radon decay releases high-energy alpha radiation that damages lung tissue cellular DNA, making it the leading environmental cause of lung cancer in non-smokers (over 21,000 U.S. deaths annually).

Radon causes no immediate symptoms such as coughing or eye irritation. Instead, inhaled solid radioactive particles lodge deep in lung passageways, emitting alpha radiation that causes long-term cellular mutation over months and years of cumulative exposure.
Testing Protocols

What is the difference between 48-hour and long-term testing?

Core Answer: 48-hour continuous digital testing provides an immediate, legally certified baseline essential for real estate escrows, while 90+ day alpha-track kits measure multi-season annual averages.

Continuous Radon Monitors (CRMs) capture hourly concentration curves with barometric pressure and motion tamper auditing. Long-term testing averages out seasonal heating and cooling cycles over several months.
Testing Protocols

What are "Closed-Building Conditions" and why are they mandatory?

Core Answer: All exterior doors and windows must remain closed for 12 hours prior to testing and throughout the entire 48-hour measurement window to simulate realistic worst-case building conditions.

Normal entry and exit is permitted, and standard central air heating/cooling operates normally. Whole-house fans and open windows invalidate tests by temporarily diluting indoor air with outdoor air.
Mitigation Engineering

How does an active sub-slab depressurization (ASD) system work?

Core Answer: A sub-slab cavity is connected to a continuously operating exterior exhaust fan, reversing the pressure relationship so soil gas is captured beneath the floor and expelled safely above the roofline.

The system draws very little power (similar to a standard 60W lightbulb) and operates silently 24/7. A U-tube manometer gauge mounted on the pipe in the basement confirms continuous vacuum operation.
Mitigation Engineering

Can homes with unfinished dirt crawlspaces be mitigated?

Core Answer: Yes. Crawlspaces are mitigated using Sub-Membrane Depressurization (SMD) by sealing a heavy-duty reinforced vapor barrier over the dirt and venting sub-membrane soil gas outdoors.

A high-mil reinforced membrane is batten-sealed to perimeter foundation walls and interior support piers. Perforated suction piping beneath the plastic membrane continuously routes gases to the exterior fan.
Real Estate & Escrow

What happens if test results exceed 4.0 pCi/L during a home purchase?

Core Answer: An elevated reading gives the buyer the contractual right to request seller mitigation, negotiate an escrow repair holdback ($1,500-$2,500), or exit the contract with earnest money protected.

Radon is one of the most straightforward issues to resolve in real estate. ClearGround provides immediate fixed-price mitigation engineering scopes that allow closings to proceed smoothly without transaction delays.
Preparation & Care

How often should a property with a mitigation system be re-tested?

Core Answer: The EPA and NRPP recommend conducting a certified 48-hour continuous re-test at least once every two years (biennially) to verify ongoing system performance under live operating conditions.

Homeowners should also glance at their U-tube manometer monthly. As long as the colored liquid remains visibly offset (between 1.2 and 1.8 inches), the fan is drawing adequate vacuum pressure.
