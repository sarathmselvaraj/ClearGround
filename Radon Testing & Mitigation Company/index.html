Why Radon Testing Matters for Every Property

Radon is a naturally occurring, odorless, colorless radioactive gas created by the uranium breakdown in underlying rock and soil. Because it possesses neither scent nor color, testing is the only reliable way to know if soil gas is concentrating inside living areas. Public health authorities emphasize that long-term exposure to elevated concentrations significantly raises indoor health risks.

Building Science

How Radon Enters a Building

Indoor radon accumulation is governed by fundamental building physics. Understanding the pathways and atmospheric pressure dynamics helps identify why levels fluctuate.

A

The Thermal Stack Effect

Warm indoor air naturally rises and vents through upper levels, creating a gentle negative pressure differential at the foundation floor that pulls soil gases upward.

B

Foundation Shrinkage & Expansion Cracks

Hairline fissures where concrete slabs meet foundation footers offer low-resistance migration channels for gas escaping the aggregate gravel bed.

C

Unsealed Sump Basins & Floor Drains

Open perimeter drainage pits directly connect indoor air to sub-surface moisture and soil gas reservoirs if unsealed by gas-tight gaskets.

D

Exposed Soil in Dirt Crawlspaces

Unsealed crawlspaces lack an impermeable physical boundary, allowing high volumes of radon and moisture to enter floor joists and ductwork.

Foundation Infiltration Mechanics

Depressurization reverses this pressure flow to vent gas harmlessly outdoors.

Understanding Your Radon Results

EPA ACTION LEVEL (4.0 – 9.9 pCi/L)
0.4 (Outdoor Air) 1.3 (Avg Indoor) 2.7 (WHO Target) 4.0 (EPA Action Level) 10.0+ (Elevated) 20.0+

Exposure Interpretation

ELEVATED: At or above the EPA federal action guideline of 4.0 pCi/L. Follow-up evaluation or mitigation is advised.

Public health agencies suggest keeping indoor radon as low as reasonably achievable, ideally below 2.0 pCi/L.

Permanent Engineering

How Radon Mitigation Works

Professional radon mitigation does not merely mask the gas—it actively reverses the pressure relationship between your foundation and the soil beneath it.

Through Active Soil Depressurization (ASD), our technicians excavate a small collection pit beneath the lowest concrete slab. A sealed PVC vent line connects this pit to a continuously operating, low-wattage centrifugal fan mounted outside the living envelope.

The fan draws soil gas from beneath the entire floor footprint before it can enter through foundation seams, routing it safely above the roofline where it disperses harmlessly into the atmosphere.
