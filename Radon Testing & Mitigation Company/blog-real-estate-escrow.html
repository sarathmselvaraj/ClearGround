Radon Testing During a Home Purchase: Escrow & Contingency Guide

By ClearGround Escrow Team • Published: Sep 10, 2026 • 6 Min Read • Real Estate Due Diligence

A pending real estate contract is high-stakes and deadline-driven. Discovering an elevated radon reading does not have to jeopardize a closing, provided you understand contingency timing, certified testing rules, and mitigation holdbacks.

1. The Radon Contingency Clause

When submitting an offer on a residential property, your purchase agreement should include a specific environmental or radon inspection contingency clause. This clause grants the buyer the legal right to place certified continuous radon monitors on the property during the standard 7-to-14 day due diligence window.

If the continuous 48-hour average test reading comes back at or above the EPA Federal Action Guideline of 4.0 pCi/L, the contingency gives the buyer the contractual right to either request seller mitigation, negotiate an escrow repair credit, or terminate the contract with earnest money fully refunded.

2. The Critical Importance of Tamper Protection

In a real estate transaction, test integrity must be airtight. Standard charcoal mail-in canisters can be tampered with or moved into fresh outdoor air. Professional continuous monitors (CRMs) feature multi-axis tilt accelerometers, continuous hourly barometric logging, and temperature sensors.

Any unauthorized movement of the machine or sudden surge of fresh outdoor air caused by open windows immediately logs a timestamped tamper warning flag on the final escrow report, ensuring total legal transparency for both buyers and lenders.

3. Repair Resolution vs. Escrow Credit

When elevated levels are discovered, parties generally pursue one of two resolution mechanisms:

  • Seller Mitigation Prior to Closing: The seller hires a licensed mitigation specialist who installs an active sub-slab depressurization system and completes a certified post-mitigation re-test demonstrating levels < 2.0 pCi/L before final loan signing.
  • Escrow Holdback Credit: The seller credits the buyer the estimated cost ($1,500 - $2,500) at closing, allowing the buyer to select their preferred installer and supervise installation after possession.
