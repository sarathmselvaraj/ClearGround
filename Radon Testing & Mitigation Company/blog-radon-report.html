Understanding a Radon Test Report: Measurements, Curves & Actions

By Dr. Marcus Vance, Ph.D. • Published: Aug 28, 2026 • 5 Min Read • EPA Action Guidelines

A certified digital radon test report contains much more than a single numerical summary. Understanding the hourly concentration curve, environmental sensor overlays, and EPA action levels empowers you to make informed decisions.

1. Understanding pCi/L and Action Thresholds

In the United States, radon concentrations are quantified in picocuries per liter of air (pCi/L), representing the rate of radioactive alpha decay events occurring in each liter of air.

  • Below 2.0 pCi/L: Low Risk. Typical ambient outdoor levels average approximately 0.4 pCi/L. Levels below 2.0 pCi/L require no remedial action.
  • 2.0 to 3.9 pCi/L: Moderate Risk. Public health agencies recommend considering mitigation, particularly if bedrooms or home offices occupy the basement.
  • 4.0 pCi/L and Above: EPA Action Level. Federal guidelines strongly advise installing an active mitigation system to reduce lung cancer risks.

2. Reading the Hourly Concentration Curve

Continuous digital monitors record readings every 60 minutes. It is completely normal to observe natural diurnal spikes (radon is often higher between midnight and 6:00 AM due to lower outdoor temperatures driving thermal stack pressure). The crucial metric evaluated in legal and escrow decisions is the 48-hour continuous overall weighted average, not an isolated single-hour spike.

3. Sensor Verification & Tamper Indicators

ClearGround reports include integrated environmental verification charts displaying concurrent barometric pressure (inHg), ambient room temperature (°F), and relative humidity (%). A sudden drop in radon accompanied by a sharp temperature plunge indicates windows were opened, alerting the inspector to test invalidation.
