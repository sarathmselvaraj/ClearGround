How Radon Mitigation Systems Work: Sub-Slab Depressurization

By Elena Rostova, PE • Published: Sep 18, 2026 • 7 Min Read • Structural ASD Engineering

Active Soil Depressurization (ASD) is universally recognized by the EPA, NRPP, and ASHRAE as the most reliable, permanent engineering technique for bringing elevated indoor radon levels well below 2.0 pCi/L.

1. The Core Mechanic: Sub-Slab Suction Cavity

An ASD system begins by creating a small collection pit (approximately 5 to 10 gallons of sub-slab aggregate excavated) through the lowest basement or ground-level slab. A heavy-duty, sealed 3-inch or 4-inch Schedule 40 PVC vent pipe is inserted into this cavity and hermetically sealed to the concrete using polyurethane elastomer sealants.

Rather than allowing soil gas to build upward hydrostatic pressure against basement floors and perimeter expansion seams, the collection cavity creates an intentional low-resistance pocket beneath the foundation footprint.

2. The Inline Centrifugal Exhaust Fan

Connected to the PVC riser is a continuously operating, weather-sealed centrifugal radon exhaust fan. By building code and EPA safety standards, this fan is never installed inside the living envelope. It is always located outside on an exterior wall, inside an unconditioned garage, or within the attic.

If any minor leak were ever to develop in the exhaust piping downstream of the fan, locating the fan outside ensures that exhaust gas under positive pressure is released outdoors rather than leaking back into interior living spaces.

3. The U-Tube Manometer: Visual Diagnostic Check

Every certified radon mitigation system includes a clear U-tube manometer mounted on the vertical PVC pipe in the basement. This simple, non-electric diagnostic tool uses colored oil to visually show the differential vacuum pressure created by the fan.

When both columns of fluid are level at zero, the fan is off or has lost electrical power. When one column rises higher than the other (typically displaying 1.2 to 2.2 inches of water column pressure), the system is actively holding sub-slab depressurization.

4. EPA Roofline Discharge Standards

To prevent exhausted radon gas from re-entering the home through upper windows, soffit vents, or neighboring air intakes, strict architectural clearance rules are applied:

  • The exhaust discharge must terminate at least 10 feet above ground level.
  • The vent opening must extend at least 12 inches above the nearest roofline surface.
  • The discharge point must remain at least 10 feet horizontally away from any operable window, door, or mechanical intake.
