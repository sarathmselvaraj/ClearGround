Understanding Short-Term vs Long-Term Radon Testing

By David Chen, CVI • Published: Sep 25, 2026 • 5 Min Read • EPA Protocol Guidelines

Because indoor radon levels fluctuate hour-by-hour with weather conditions, barometric pressure swings, and furnace cycles, selecting the proper testing protocol is essential to getting accurate, actionable results.

1. What Is a 48-Hour Short-Term Test?

Short-term testing is the industry standard for real estate transactions, initial property assessments, and post-mitigation performance checks. Continuous Radon Monitors (CRMs) record measurements at hourly intervals over an unbroken minimum period of 48 hours.

To ensure the validity of short-term tests, closed-building conditions are mandatory. All exterior doors and windows must remain closed for 12 hours prior to the test and throughout the entire 48-hour monitoring window, simulating peak concentration dynamics under routine building HVAC operation.

2. What Is a Long-Term Test (90 to 365 Days)?

Long-term tests utilize passive alpha-track detectors or electret ion chambers deployed in the living area for a duration ranging from 90 days up to a full calendar year. Because they span multiple meteorological seasons, long-term tests capture both summer air conditioning periods and winter heating seasons.

While long-term testing delivers the ultimate annual average exposure figure, it is unsuitable for real estate contingencies where buyers and sellers typically have an inspection objection window of just 7 to 14 days.

3. Direct Comparison: Key Diagnostic Criteria

Metric 48-Hour Continuous (Short-Term) 90+ Day Alpha-Track (Long-Term)
Turnaround Time 48 hours (immediate digital readout) 3 to 12 months + lab mail-in
Real Estate Legal Acceptance 100% Accepted in Escrow Too slow for closing deadlines
Tamper Detection Sensors detect movement, temp, pressure None (passive canister)
Data Resolution Hourly curve & barometric overlay Single aggregate average

4. Which Test Should You Order?

If you are buying or selling a home, investigating a potential radon issue quickly, or verifying a newly installed mitigation system, order a 48-hour continuous digital test. If you already own your home, have tested in the low-to-moderate range (between 2.0 and 3.9 pCi/L), and want to understand your year-round baseline before deciding on mitigation, a long-term alpha-track test provides excellent seasonal insight.
