Why Follow-Up Testing Matters After Mitigation

By Elena Rostova, PE • Published: Aug 14, 2026 • 4 Min Read • System Lifecycle & Verification

Installing a radon mitigation system is a monumental step toward permanent indoor health. However, because ASD systems operate 24 hours a day, 365 days a year against subterranean soil moisture and settling foundations, periodic verification is mandatory.

1. Environmental Factors That Alter Pressure Fields

Sub-slab depressurization relies on an established pressure communication field beneath the concrete. Over time, several common domestic changes can disrupt this equilibrium:

  • Foundation Settling & New Shrinkage Cracks: Natural thermal expansion and ground settlement can open new unsealed fissures in slab joints.
  • HVAC Upgrades & Bathroom Exhausts: High-efficiency furnaces or powerful kitchen range hoods alter interior building negative pressure dynamics.
  • Plumbing Alterations & Additions: Sump pump replacements or new drain rough-ins can puncture the sub-slab vacuum barrier.

2. The EPA 2-Year Biennial Re-Test Standard

The EPA and NRPP recommend that every home with an operating mitigation system undergo a certified 48-hour continuous digital re-test at least once every two years. This biennial audit guarantees that the system is continuing to keep levels below the 2.0 pCi/L benchmark under live operating conditions.

3. Routine Monthly Manometer Checks

Homeowners should glance at their U-tube manometer once a month. The liquid columns should remain visibly offset. If both columns level out at 0.0 inches, check the dedicated circuit breaker or exterior GFCI outlet before calling a technician.
